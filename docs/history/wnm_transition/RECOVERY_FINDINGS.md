# Parameter recovery — closed-loop panel (2026-09-07)

This is the first, diagnostic panel from transition step 5. Responses are drawn
from the K12 mixture and fitted back with it, so there is no surrogate error: it
cannot say whether the mixture approximates the observer, only whether the
wiring, gradients, and objective work. The subsequent actual-DM n=100 panel is
now complete for all four retained objectives; see the repository `HISTORY.md` and the
objective-specific `*FINDINGS.md` files. Statements below about what this
precursor alone could not settle retain their original scope.

Generated evidence under `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT` is external and is not
shipped with or expected in a normal checkout.

Library: `model_fit_to_data/recovery.py`. Protocol: `model_fit_to_data/run_recovery_panel.py`.
Tests: `tests/test_recovery.py`.

Every number below is read from `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/{noise_free,empirical,sample_size}_{rows.csv,summary.json}`
in this directory, regenerated by:

```bash
JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu python model_fit_to_data/run_recovery_panel.py --panel noise_free   --out "$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery"
JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu python model_fit_to_data/run_recovery_panel.py --panel empirical    --out "$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery" --n-replicates 5 --n-trials 4000
JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu python model_fit_to_data/run_recovery_panel.py --panel sample_size  --out "$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery" --n-replicates 1 --trial-counts 400 2000 10000
```

Settings are recorded in each summary's `settings` block, checkpoint digest
included. The first run of this panel was done interactively and only its
conclusions were written down; the numbers below were re-derived through the
script, and the 4,000-trial table reproduces that first run exactly.

## What was asked

The plan requires three questions be kept apart, because one error number
conflates them: did the **search** find the basin, does the **objective** prefer
other parameters, and does the **target's construction** move the optimum.

## Noise-free reference

Fitting the model's own smoothed asymmetry curve at the generating parameters —
no sampling noise, no KDE, no empirical feature weighting — recovers them to
1.5e-4 in log ratio at worst:

| parameter | true | fitted | log ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `sd_feat1_c0` | 15 | 15.0019 | +1.24e-4 |
| `sd_feat2_c0` | 45 | 45.0016 | +3.57e-5 |
| `sd_feat1_c1` | 60 | 59.9986 | −2.31e-5 |
| `sd_feat2_c1` | 20 | 20.0029 | +1.46e-4 |
| `sd_spat` | 25 | 24.9995 | −2.06e-5 |

16 starts, 14 converged, loss at the fit −2.4e-07 against 0 at the truth — a
difference of about two float32 ULPs, reported as a tie.

**What this licenses.** At *this generating vector*, the density objective's
optimum sits at the truth and the best of 16 starts reaches it. That is one
interior point, so it is not a statement that the objective is identifiable
across the parameter space, and it is emphatically not a statement that the
search is reliable: the spread across converged starts is 1.99, with most starts
stopping at losses between 0.03 and 1.99. The best start finds the optimum; a
typical one does not.

## Against an empirical target

Five replicates at 4,000 trials per condition, through the real empirical target
a subject's data would build:

| parameter | median log ratio | RMSE (log ratio) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `sd_feat1_c0` | +0.141 | 0.141 |
| `sd_feat2_c0` | +0.006 | 0.087 |
| `sd_feat1_c1` | −0.051 | 0.073 |
| `sd_feat2_c1` | −0.051 | 0.102 |
| `sd_spat` | +0.044 | 0.074 |

No replicate hit a bound. Per replicate, the worst parameter is `sd_feat1_c0`
three times and `sd_feat2_c0` twice, at 0.134 to 0.186 in absolute log ratio.

## Sample size

One replicate per trial count, so these are single draws, not trends:

| trials/condition | worst parameter | its log ratio | at a bound? |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 400 | `sd_feat1_c0` | −1.792 | **yes**, `sd_feat1_c0@low` |
| 2,000 | `sd_feat2_c1` | −0.095 | no |
| 10,000 | `sd_feat1_c0` | −0.078 | no |

The 400-trial fit railed at the low `sd_feat` bound of 2.5, so its −1.792 is the
distance to the bound rather than a recovery error; it says the target carried
too little information to locate that parameter at all, which is a different
finding from a large error.

Between 2,000 and 10,000 the worst error is 0.095 and 0.078. **One replicate at
each count cannot distinguish a plateau from sampling scatter**, and the
5-replicate panel above shows per-replicate worst errors spanning 0.134–0.186 at
a *fixed* count — a spread comparable to the 2,000-to-10,000 difference. So the
honest reading is that error falls sharply out of the very-small-sample regime
and is somewhere in the 5–15% range by 2,000 trials, with the shape of the tail
not established. Establishing it needs replicates at each count.

## Attribution

In all replicates at every trial count the search reached a loss *better* than
the loss at the truth, by −0.007 to −0.212. No replicate was diagnosed
`search_failed`.

**What that does and does not show.** It shows the returned parameters are not
worse than the generating ones under the realised empirical objective, which
rules out the simplest search failure: stopping short of the truth's basin. It
does **not** on its own attribute the residual to the target's construction.
Two other explanations survive it:

- **Finite-trial sampling variation.** The empirical target is built from a
  finite sample, so its optimum is a random variable scattered about the truth
  even if the estimator is unbiased. A loss better than the truth's is exactly
  what that predicts, and this panel does not separate it from a systematic
  offset. The consistent sign on `sd_feat1_c0` (+0.141 median over five
  replicates, positive in all five) is suggestive of a systematic component, but
  five replicates is not an estimate of one.
- **A residual search gap.** Beating the truth does not make the returned point
  the objective's optimum; a better optimum may exist that no start found. The
  start spread of 1.84–1.96 on every empirical replicate says the search is far
  from exhaustive.

So the defensible statement is narrower than the first write-up's: the density
objective, the continuous search and the mixture's gradients are wired correctly
and mutually consistent, and the residual error at 4,000 trials is not explained
by the search failing to reach the truth. Which of finite-sample scatter,
estimator bias, or an unfound optimum dominates is open.

## What this does not license

It does **not** license a claim about how well K12 recovers parameters from real
behaviour: this panel has no surrogate error by construction. It does not compare
K12 against the surface network, or one search against another; those are the
separate arms the plan specifies. And it is not an uncertainty estimate for a
real experiment's parameters — five replicates at one configuration, one
generating vector, one objective.

## Consequences worth carrying forward

- **The start budget matters for finding the optimum, but does not explain the
  observed density-recovery failure.**
  On the noise-free problem 2 of 16 starts failed to converge and the spread
  across converged starts was 1.99; on the empirical replicates the spread was
  1.84–1.96. The search is decided by a small number of good starts in every
  panel run. But the range panel below shows that raising 16 to 64 starts does
  not materially improve *recovery* against an empirical target — it converges
  more reliably to an optimum that is not at the truth. That does not validate
  the continuous search: the hard-case sweep below shows a separate optimization
  problem. The production choice must compare optimization gap and cost, by
  objective, including hierarchical/cached references.
- **Follow-up status.** The later actual-DM density panel, matched-KDE alignment
  run, and solution-equivalence analysis tested target construction, optimizer
  choice, and prediction equivalence over replicated cases and trial counts.
  See `DENSITY_ALIGNMENT_FINDINGS.md`,
  `DENSITY_SOLUTION_EQUIVALENCE_FINDINGS.md`, and
  `DENSITY_MATCHED_KDE_HELDOUT_FINDINGS.md`. They confirm weak feature-SD
  identification rather than reducing the result to a start-count failure.
- **A railed fit is not an error measurement.** The 400-trial point rails at the
  `sd_feat` low bound; any summary that averages it in reports the bound's
  position, not the estimator's.

# Range panel — recovery across the whole parameter space (2026-09-07)

Everything above is one generating vector. This section is the panel that varies
it: truths drawn log-uniformly across the full search bounds (`sd_feat`
2.5-200, `sd_spat` 5-200), 300 cases per cell, one replicate each, matched
across cells by `truth_digest` so a difference between cells is the cell's own
variable and nothing else.

Artifacts: `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/one_n{500,10000}_s{16,64}` (single condition),
`$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/range_n{100,500,2000,10000}` and `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/obj_density_n*_s64` (two
conditions, shared `sd_spat`), `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/legacy_*` (the pre-transition cost).
Regenerate with `run_recovery_panel.py --panel random`.

## Headline

**`sd_spat` recovers; `sd_feat` does not.** At 10,000 trials per condition,
`sd_spat` reaches r(log) 0.73-0.85 with slope 0.67-0.75 and a median fitted
value of 32.6 against a true median of 32.4 -- centrally unbiased. `sd_feat`
reaches r(log) 0.35-0.49 with slope 0.32-0.47, and only 36-40% of parameters
land within 25% of the truth.

The slope is the number to read, not the correlation: a slope near 0.45 means
fitted values are compressed toward the middle of the range by more than half,
which the correlation alone does not show.

## Why `sd_feat` fails: saturation above ~50 degrees

Fitted median by true value, single condition, 10,000 trials:

| true `sd_feat` | fitted median |
|---|---:|
| 2.5-6 | 5.5 |
| 6-12 | 8.4 |
| 12-25 | 15.0 |
| 25-50 | 30.2 |
| 50-100 | 23.1 |
| 100-196 | 46.6 |

Tracking is reasonable to about 50 degrees and collapses above it. That is what
a near-uniform response distribution looks like from the outside: the asymmetry
curve stops changing with the parameter, so 60 and 200 are not distinguishable
and the fit is pulled down.

Restricting to 2.5-50 does **not** rescue it -- r 0.40, slope 0.44, 43% within
25% -- so the failure is not only the unidentifiable tail.

## Three explanations ruled out

**It is not the start budget.** `sd_feat` recovery against an empirical target,
16 against 64 starts on matched truths:

| design | trials | starts | r(log) | slope | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 cond | 500 | 16 | 0.406 | 0.280 | 1.313 |
| 1 cond | 500 | 64 | 0.416 | 0.288 | 1.302 |
| 1 cond | 10,000 | 16 | 0.336 | 0.309 | 1.396 |
| 1 cond | 10,000 | 64 | 0.352 | 0.322 | 1.365 |
| 2 cond | 500 | 16 | 0.225 | 0.180 | 1.430 |
| 2 cond | 500 | 64 | 0.249 | 0.199 | 1.405 |
| 2 cond | 10,000 | 16 | 0.468 | 0.443 | 1.242 |
| 2 cond | 10,000 | 64 | 0.493 | 0.465 | 1.210 |

Quadrupling the starts is worth about 0.02 in correlation. Meanwhile the *same*
start counts transform the search's ability to find an optimum that is known to
exist -- `--panel start_sweep`, which refits the 40 worst-recovered cases from
`range_n10000` against a noise-free target whose optimum is at the truth with a
loss of exactly 0:

| starts | median worst \|log ratio\| | p90 | within 1% | missed the known optimum | median s/fit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 1.4779 | 2.837 | 25% | 30/40 | 2.2 |
| 64 | 0.0030 | 2.415 | 52.5% | 18/40 | 7.1 |
| 256 | 0.0003 | 0.514 | 82.5% | 7/40 | 27.2 |

Read together, these two tables are the point. The search genuinely is
start-starved -- at 16 starts it misses a known global optimum in three quarters
of hard cases, and even 256 starts misses 7 of 40 -- and improving that
does essentially nothing for recovery, because the empirical target's optimum
is not at the truth. **The noise-free sweep bounds the search; the empirical
panels bound the target. Only the second bounds what a fitted parameter means**,
and an earlier reading of this panel that called the start count the dominant
problem was generalising from the first to the second.

Artifact: `$DEMIXING_ARTIFACT_ROOT/continuous_density_4.1q/recovery/start_sweep/`. The first
runner implementation had two silent selection/construction defects. Alphabetic
truth-column order is condition-*minor* (`feat1_c0, feat1_c1, feat2_c0, ...`),
so it paired feature SDs from different conditions. After that was fixed, the
runner still kept source-row order and took the first 40 rows over the threshold,
not the 40 worst rows stated here. Both defects leave every loss finite and a
plausible summary. The artifact has been regenerated after ranking by the source
panel's worst absolute feature-SD log ratio. The production call site is pinned by
`test_the_start_sweep_uses_the_worst_cases_and_optimizer_truth_layout`.

**It is not the shared-`sd_spat` coupling.** The single-condition design has
three free parameters and no cross-condition constraint, and recovers `sd_feat`
no better -- slightly worse at 10,000 trials (r 0.35 against 0.49).

**It is not the new objective.** `density` (1 - CCC) and `density_legacy`
(MSE + 0.25*(1 - Pearson), the cost used before continuous density fitting) are
within noise of each other on matched truths at 64 starts:

| design | trials | objective | r(log) | slope | RMSE | within 25% |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 cond | 500 | density | 0.416 | 0.288 | 1.302 | 24% |
| 1 cond | 500 | density_legacy | 0.401 | 0.370 | 1.300 | 22% |
| 1 cond | 10,000 | density | 0.352 | 0.322 | 1.365 | 36% |
| 1 cond | 10,000 | density_legacy | 0.315 | 0.288 | 1.389 | 33% |
| 2 cond | 500 | density | 0.249 | 0.199 | 1.405 | 19% |
| 2 cond | 500 | density_legacy | 0.290 | 0.262 | 1.423 | 19% |
| 2 cond | 10,000 | density | 0.493 | 0.465 | 1.210 | 40% |
| 2 cond | 10,000 | density_legacy | 0.480 | 0.451 | 1.221 | 36% |

CCC is ahead on RMSE and on within-25% in seven of the eight comparisons, but
the margins are small and not uniform: at 500 trials on the two-condition design
`density_legacy` has the better correlation and slope (0.290/0.262 against
0.249/0.199), so "within noise of each other" is the honest summary rather than
"CCC wins". Neither recovers `sd_feat`. The transition did not break recovery;
recovery was never established.

## What this panel did not settle

This range panel did not isolate empirical target construction from intrinsic
identifiability. Subsequent density-alignment work applied the same KDE operator
to both branches: it reduced large-error tails but did not shift typical
recovery. The solution-equivalence check then found both genuine
non-identifiability and materially wrong selected distributions, depending on
regime. Those later results supersede this section as the current diagnosis.

The hierarchical, continuous, JAX-BADS, and cached searches were subsequently
compared on the actual-DM development panel. The selected density search is an
80-by-64 log curve cache followed by one continuous polish; see
`WNM_DENSITY_FINDINGS.md`. Hierarchical search is therefore no longer untested.

## Traps recorded

- **Orphaned workers.** `ProcessPoolExecutor` children are spawned, so their
  command line is `spawn_main`; a `pkill` on the script name misses them. An
  interrupted panel left 22 workers holding ~1 GB each for an hour, exhausted
  swap, and surfaced as unrelated `OSError: Cannot allocate memory` failures in
  five test-suite tests. Workers now set `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG`.
- **`os.replace` is not reliable on this results tree.** It is a v9fs (WSL 9p)
  mount where renaming over an existing file intermittently raises
  `PermissionError`; it killed a four-cell panel 134 fits into the third cell.
